# 07 · Price tiers for batch models

*Written by Claude Opus 5.5 · 2026-10-31 · prices from the 2026-10-31 snapshot ([`reference/batch-models/2026-10-31_batch-models.csv`](../reference/batch-models/2026-10-31_batch-models.csv), 71 `:batch` ids)*

A rubric for sorting batch models into three tiers, with two sub-bands each. Batch prices
only, US$ per million tokens. Generated by [`scripts/price_tiers.py`](../scripts/price_tiers.py)
(`python3 -I scripts/price_tiers.py <snapshot.csv> <out-prefix>`), which also writes
[`2026-10-31_price-tiers.csv`](../reference/batch-models/2026-10-31_price-tiers.csv).

## Rubric

**Primary axis: batch output price.** Boundaries sit in the widest gaps of the price ladder
(2.50 → 3.75, 7.50 → 10, 15 → 25, 37.50 → 60), so a small repricing should not move a model
across one.

| 1 Cheap | 1a | ≤ 1.00 | 17 | $0.16 – $1.50 | gpt-oss, DeepSeek/GLM flash, nano, Haiku 5.5, Luna, Flash-Lite |
| 1 Cheap | 1b | 1.25 – 2.50 | 13 | $1.30 – $5.50 | Gemini Flash, GLM 5.3, Grok 4.3, o3/o4-mini, GPT-6.1 Terra, Haiku 4.5 |

| 2 Mid | 2b | 10 – 15 | 11 | $14 – $30 | Opus 4.5 – 5.5, GPT-5.6 Sol, GPT-5.5, Kimi K3, GPT-4 Turbo |
| 3 Expensive | 3a | 25 – 37.50 | 5 | $35 – $52.50 | Fable 5 / 5.1, GPT-6 Astra, Opus 4.1 |
| 3 Expensive | 3b | 60 – 90 | 4 | $64 – $113 | GPT-5 / 5.2 / 5.4 / 5.5 Pro |

Each step up is roughly 2–3× on output price; the full span is ~800× (0.112 → 90).

**Secondary axis: batch input price.** Bands: cheap < 0.60, mid 0.60 – 3.00, expensive ≥ 3.00
(nothing is priced between 2.50 and 5.00). Output is 4–8× input for 67 of 71 models, so the
input band nearly always matches the output tier. Two models break it (flagged in bold below):

- `x-ai/grok-4.3` (1.00 / 2.00, ratio 2): cheap output, mid input. On input-heavy jobs it
  costs like a 2a model.
- `openai/gpt-4-turbo` (5.00 / 15.00, ratio 3): mid output, expensive input. On input-heavy
  jobs it costs like 3a.

The ratio also matters inside a tier. OpenAI's GPT-5 line runs at 8× (cheap input for its
output price), so `gpt-5` / `gpt-5.1` (0.625 / 5) are cheaper on input-heavy work than the
Sonnet/Sol models at 1.00 / 5. DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (3×) is the reverse.

## Which axis to tier on

**Tier by the job's blended cost, not by output price alone.** For the notebook's reference job
(4.5M input + 0.5M output, 9:1, the shape of bulk labelling), input is most of the bill: at a
5× ratio input is 64% of cost. The tiers still separate cleanly on job cost except at the 1b/2a
edge, where they overlap ($5.50 for Grok 4.3 vs $5.25 for Mistral Medium 3.5). For
generation-heavy jobs (output ≥ input) the output tier alone is a good enough guide.
